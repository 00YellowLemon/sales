# Enterprise Sales Qualification Notes

*Based on Y Combinator's Startup School talk by Pete Koomen*

## Step 3: Qualification (The First Call)
- **Primary Rule:** Your job on the first call is **NOT to sell your product**. Do not dive straight into your pitch.
- **Goals of the First Call:**
  1. **Qualify the Prospect:** Figure out whether they have the problem you're trying to solve, the budget, and the decision-making authority to buy the product.
  2. **Schedule a follow-up call** for a product demo (if qualified).
- **The Mindset Shift on Sales:**
  - Founders often view the target company as a "big monolithic entity" and the sales process as "adversarial" (where the goal is a perfect pitch to break down defenses).
  - *Reality:* You are almost always selling to an *individual human*, not a monolith. Humans are easier to understand.
  - Sales is not adversarial. It's about deeply understanding a customer's problem and helping them solve it.
- **Listen > Pitch:** Great salespeople spend most of their time listening, as it is the best way to understand the problem.
- **Crucial Qualification Questions (Scripting Nuances):**
  - *What made you decide to take this call?*
  - *Tell me about this problem.*
  - *How long have you had it?*
  - *How bad is it?*
  - *Who else does it affect?*
  - *How do you quantify the impact?*
  - *Why haven't you solved it already?*
  - *What's your budget for solving it?*
  - *How does your organization buy software?*
  - *Who makes the buying decision?*
  - *Who else will need to weigh in on this decision?*
- **Outcome:** Sometimes you discover they don't have the problem, don't care enough to solve it, or lack budget. *This is a great outcome.* You just saved both parties time and can focus energy on better prospects. If they do have a solvable problem, you've earned the opportunity to show them how your product works.
