# B2B Sales for Startups: Strategies, Tactics & Tradecraft - Session 1
**Speaker:** Kent Summers (Founded/exited 3 startups, teaches B2B sales at MIT & Harvard Business School, Kellogg, Thai Boston, etc.)

## 1. Core Mindset and Concepts

### Revenue is Everything
* As a founder, you have two things to worry about: **revenue** and **everything else**.
* If you can get revenue flowing, everything else becomes manageable. If you cannot acquire customers cost-effectively, the game is over.

### What is B2B (Enterprise) Sales?
* **Definition:** A smaller business selling to a larger business.
* **Complexity:** You are solving complex goals, problems, or needs. If it wasn't complex, it wouldn't be a problem or it would already be solved.
* **Innovation:** Solving problems better, faster, or cheaper by leveraging innovation.
* **Value & Price:** Offerings are typically higher-priced. The price isn't necessarily based on COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) but on the scale of the problem you're solving (e.g., solving a $1 problem for 1 million people = a $1 million problem).
* **Unknowns & Uncertainty:** B2B sales always involve unknowns.
* **Displacement:** Your solution usually displaces something or someone (inside or outside the company).
* **Team Decision:** Rarely does a single person evaluate, decide, and write the check. Decision-making is typically distributed among 4-6 people.
* **Costly Process:** B2B sales has a long and expensive sales cycle. It requires significant investment in time and opportunity cost.

### The True Nature of Sales
* **It's Not Chasing:** Sales is *not* about chasing people down to close deals.
* **It's Helping:** Sales is about helping people achieve a goal, solve a problem, or satisfy a need. If you are successful in helping them, transactions will naturally occur as an outcome.
* **Attitude & Behavior:** You only control your attitude and behavior. These are your leading indicators of success.
* **Not Picking Winners:** Do not try to pick winners and losers early on. Trying to guess who will buy leads to wasted time and missed opportunities. Sales is feeding a process.
* **Learned Skill:** Salespeople aren't born; it's a learned skill through practice and repetition. Technical founders can be excellent at sales.
* **Failure-Driven Game:** B2B sales is a numbers game with a 90%+ failure rate. Success is not about failure avoidance, but **failure recovery**.
    * "SW³ / N" = Some Will, Some Won't, So What? Next!
* **Qualifying vs. Disqualifying:** Don't "always be qualifying." Good prospects are easy to spot. Effective sales is about **disqualifying** people with the appearance of real interest, helping them opt-out early.

## 2. The Founder as the First Salesperson

### Why the Founder?
* Has intimate knowledge of the offering, the customer, the need, and the value delivered.
* Has a unique vision that helps overcome distorted realities.
* Must understand firsthand the blocking issues preventing customers from buying (crucial for finding Product-Market Fit).
* Feedback directly fuels the product roadmap.

### When to Hire a Salesperson?
* **Do NOT hire a professional salesperson to figure out the sales dilemma.** This is an accelerant to failure.
* **When to hire:** Hire your first salesperson *only* when you have figured out the formula and can say, "Go out and get me 100 more exactly like these 12." Do not hire them to figure out who the customer is.

## 3. The Sales Toolkit

### The Customer Profile
Your profile focuses your daily sales activity. It consists of three concentric rings:

1.  **Company Profile (Outer Ring):** Industry, type of company, size, and geography. (e.g., Financial services, family offices, >$100M under management, within 25 miles of Boston). *Acid Test:* You should be able to get a specific list of street addresses from this profile.
2.  **Buyer Profile (Middle Ring):** The person inside the company with the most to gain or lose. Determined by job title/role and specific unmet needs (expressed as a use case).
3.  **Decision-Making Unit (Center):** The group that evaluates, influences, and holds the checkbook.

**Rules for Building the Profile:**
* Gather **facts, not opinions**. Don't ask "Would you use this?" Ask "Do you currently do this?"
* Focus on facts related to **pain and cost**, not theoretical gains.
* Focus on a **single use case** initially to avoid diluting focus.
* **Ultimate Validation:** Receiving purchase orders from different companies to solve a similar problem. (This is also the true definition of an MVP).

### The Sales Pipeline (Funnel)
The process from lead to close, used for time management and focus.

1.  **Leads:** Raw material.
2.  **Suspects (MQLs - Marketing Qualified Leads):** Filtered leads that meet your company profile criteria. (They want to know "what kind of cookies you have").
3.  **Prospects (SQLs - Sales Qualified Leads):** Contact has verified that the buyer profile and interest exist. (You verified they have a $10 budget and are deciding between Oreos and Thin Mints).
4.  **Opportunities:** Qualified prospects actively engaged in your sales process. You are developing the decision-making unit.
5.  **Customers:** Closed deals.

**Pipeline Dynamics:**
* **Conversion Metrics:** The percentage of people moving from one stage to the next. These should intentionally increase left to right (e.g., 10% suspect to prospect -> 15% prospect to opportunity -> 33% opportunity to close). If they don't, your criteria are too loose.
* **Triage vs. Attrition:** Triage is you removing people because they aren't qualified for your time. Attrition is them removing you.
* **Team Involvement:** Keep your team away from Suspects/Prospects. Engage your team at the Opportunity stage to expand relationships beyond 1-on-1 (connect your team with their team).

## 4. Lead Generation & Websites

### The Role of the Website in B2B
* B2B websites **do not close deals**; they only generate leads. They are actually very good at *preventing* deals if poorly designed.
* **Mistakes:** Overbuilding the website, explaining excruciating detail, focusing on features/tools instead of the "why" and the outcome.
* **Goal:** You have 30 seconds to make it clear what you do, who it's for, and make them curious enough to contact you. Provide benefits supported by features. Your goal is a 5-minute visit that causes them to return later in the context of a project.

### Inbound vs. Outbound Lead Generation
* **Outbound (Direct Marketing, SDRs):** You initiate contact. Their need/priority is not established. Costs grow proportionally with revenue.
* **Inbound (SEO, PPC):** They initiate contact (searching for a solution). Requires optimization around the *problem* you solve, not the solution. Upfront heavy lifting, but cost of sales decreases over time. Recommended long-term strategy.

## 5. Execution & Tradecraft (Agent Skills)

### Communication Tactics
* **Be Brief:** Diminishing returns on attention happen fast. Keep interactions narrow and well-defined. Don't stuff multiple sales steps into one interaction.
* **Never Sell Over Email:** If someone emails you a problem, do not solve it via email. Reply: "Sounds like we can help. Send me days/times you are available for a call." Drive the interaction to a real-time conversation (Zoom/Phone).
* **Use "We", not "I":** Appear bigger and more professional than a single individual.
* **Respect Calendars:** You are ambushing people's time. Be sensitive, ask for 15-20 minutes, and always **add value** (insight, guidance) in every interaction so they talk to you because you're helpful, not out of obligation.
* **Tone:** Be serious about the business, but don't take yourself too seriously. Maintain an "office casual" behavioral tone.
* **Provide Off-Ramps:** Give people ways to opt-out so you can solicit clear, actionable buying (or non-buying) signals.

### Managing Opportunities & Time
* **Touch Everyone:** Early on, all leads look the same. You must touch them all to determine who is real.
* **Trust Instincts:** You will never have complete information. Move forward or triage based on gut feeling. Don't dwell on lost deals.
* **The Refrigerator Model for Timing:** Manage your pipeline based on urgency:
    * *Counter/Highly Perishable:* Deciding in days/weeks. Needs 2-3 touches a week.
    * *Refrigerator:* Deciding in months. Needs 1-2 touches a month.
    * *Freezer:* No active decision date (e.g., next year's budget). Touch base every 3-6 months.
* **Balanced Activity:** Do not spend 100% of your time on the top 3 deals. Spend 50% on top deals and 50% developing the rest of the funnel. This prevents the "groundhog day" rollercoaster of cash flow.

### Handling the Sale
* **Don't Educate/Evangelize:** If you have to teach them the basics of what you do, or if the business case isn't obvious, they are not qualified for your time. Convert knowledgeable people; don't train the ill-informed for free.
* **The "Peter the Pocket" (Competitive Intel):** If someone is asking great questions but won't commit, ask about their actual project timeline. Draw a line: "To help further with your specific data, we recommend a $5k-$20k pilot." If they balk, they are just picking your pocket for info.

## 6. Building the Buyer-Seller Relationship

Salespeople fall into categories based on attitude and behavior:
1.  *The Pusher:* Highly efficient, highly aggressive, lousy performance.
2.  *The Dreamer:* Bloated pipeline, hoping for closes, afraid to triage.
3.  *The Mechanic:* Skilled, recognizes lack of signals, moves on.
4.  *The Facilitator (Ideal):* Helps prospects sell themselves or opt out.

### The Four Pillars of the Relationship
Do not execute these backwards!

1.  **Empathy:** Convince them you truly understand their unique needs and pain. (Don't jump straight into "fix-it" mode).
2.  **Trust:** Prove you have their best interests in mind (even if it means pointing them to a competitor).
3.  **Value:** Express the value of the offering in *their* terms, in *their* world, based on *their* outcome measures.
4.  **Competence:** Only after they buy into the value do you earn the right to talk about your product, features, capabilities, and team.

*Jumping straight to competence/features without empathy/trust is viewed as arrogant, aggressive, and dismissive of their unique circumstances.*
