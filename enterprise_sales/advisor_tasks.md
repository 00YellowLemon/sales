# Enterprise Sales Advisor Agent - Responsibilities and Required Context

Based on the Enterprise Sales playbook, this document outlines the required context, high-level strategies, and granular actions that an Enterprise Sales Advisor Agent would use to review the founder's work and provide strategic guidance throughout the process of closing their first Enterprise customers.

## Required Information from the Founder

To provide highly personalized and effective advice, the Advisor Agent requires the founder to provide specific context and artifacts at each phase of the sales process.

### Phase 1: Prospecting
*   **Product Context:** A clear description of the product being built and the core problem it solves.
*   **Draft Sales Hypothesis:** The founder's initial attempt at formulating the "Customer X has problem Y" hypothesis.
*   **Target Criteria:** Proposed filtering criteria (e.g., technologies used, company size, industry) for finding prospects.
*   **Target Lists:** Initial lists of target companies and the specific human contacts within them.

### Phase 2: Outreach
*   **Email Drafts:** Drafts of cold emails or outreach messages.
*   **Inbound Strategy:** Ideas or plans for technical content, self-serve demos, or forums the founder intends to use.
*   **Warm Lead Context:** Information on potential shared connections or warm introductions.
*   **Target Audience Profile:** Details on who the founder is actually getting on the phone with (to check for anti-patterns).

### Phase 3: Qualification (The First Call)
*   **Call Preparation:** A list of qualification questions the founder plans to ask.
*   **Call Notes/Transcripts:** Detailed notes or transcripts from the first call with a prospect.
*   **Founder's Assessment:** The founder's evaluation of the prospect's problem severity, budget, and decision-making authority based on the call.

### Phase 4: Delivering a Great Demo
*   **Prospect Pain Points:** Summary of the prospect's main problems identified during qualification.
*   **Demo Script/Flow:** An outline or script of how the founder plans to conduct the demo.
*   **Customization Plan:** Details on how the founder plans to tailor the demo to the specific prospect.
*   **Magic Moment Ideas:** The feature or interaction the founder plans to highlight as the "magic moment."

### Phase 5: Pricing
*   **Value Assessment:** Information gathered on how much the problem is costing the prospect's company.
*   **Proposed Pricing:** The specific price point or structure the founder is considering pitching.
*   **Champion Materials:** Drafts of the 1-page pricing overview or ROI justification document intended for the internal champion.
*   **Prospect Reaction:** Feedback on how the prospect reacted to the initial pricing discussion.

### Phase 6: Closing the Deal
*   **Procurement Process:** Details gathered from the prospect about their specific buying process (legal, security, compliance steps).
*   **Current Blockers:** Any friction or objections encountered during the legal or security review.
*   **Contract Drafts:** Simplified legal documents or order forms being sent to the prospect.

### Phase 7: Managing the Implementation
*   **Implementation Plan:** Draft of the shared roadmap or project tracking document for deployment.
*   **Task Ownership:** A list of implementation tasks and who owns them (on both the startup and customer sides).
*   **Success Metric:** The defined milestone that represents "habitual usage" for this specific customer.

## High-Level Phases (Advisor Strategies)

*   **Phase 1: Prospecting**
    *   Review and refine the founder's sales hypothesis to ensure it is narrowly defined and actionable.
    *   Guide the founder in selecting the right filtering criteria to identify high-potential companies.
*   **Phase 2: Outreach**
    *   Critique cold email drafts to ensure they are highly personalized, concise, and have a clear ask.
    *   Act as a guardrail against the "talking to anyone who will take your call" anti-pattern by validating that the targeted prospects actually match the ideal customer profile (e.g., warning against selling top-down enterprise software to startups).
*   **Phase 3: Qualification (The First Call)**
    *   Review call notes to ensure the founder prioritized listening over pitching the product.
    *   Advise the founder on whether a prospect is truly qualified (problem, budget, authority) or if they should be disqualified to save time.
*   **Phase 4: Delivering a Great Demo**
    *   Review the demo script to ensure it is structured as a compelling story focused on solving the prospect's specific problems, avoiding a generic "feature tour".
    *   Provide feedback on the level of personalization and the planned "magic moments".
*   **Phase 5: Pricing**
    *   Advise against underpricing or giving the product away for free just to get feedback.
    *   Review pricing proposals to ensure they reflect the value of solving the prospect's problem and recommend leaning towards higher initial prices to test conviction.
    *   Review and refine materials intended to help the internal champion sell the product to their stakeholders.
*   **Phase 6: Closing the Deal**
    *   Review the prospect's procurement process and suggest ways to parallelize tasks (like security questionnaires) to accelerate closing.
    *   Advise the founder on how to effectively activate and lean on their internal champion to overcome internal hurdles.
*   **Phase 7: Managing the Implementation**
    *   Review the implementation plan before contract signing to ensure it is detailed, actionable, and has clear accountability on both sides.
    *   Advise on project management best practices to ensure the customer reaches the critical milestone of habitual product usage.

## Granular Actions & Tasks

### Prospecting
*   [ ] Review the founder's draft sales hypothesis and provide specific edits for clarity and targeting.
*   [ ] Evaluate the founder's proposed filtering criteria and suggest additional signals (e.g., specific technologies, hiring patterns).
*   [ ] Review the initial list of target companies and human contacts for relevance to the hypothesis.

### Outreach
*   [ ] Edit cold email drafts to remove fluff, clarify the ask, and verify it doesn't sound like a mass email.
*   [ ] Review the list of scheduled calls to identify and flag potential "bad fit" prospects (e.g., startups for enterprise solutions, bottom-up targets for top-down software).
*   [ ] Provide strategic feedback on the founder's inbound demand generation plan (content, forums, conferences).

### Qualification (The First Call)
*   [ ] Review the list of qualification questions the founder intends to ask; suggest missing critical questions (e.g., budget, decision-making process).
*   [ ] Analyze first-call notes to assess the talk-to-listen ratio; provide feedback if the founder is pitching too early.
*   [ ] Provide a definitive recommendation (Qualify vs. Disqualify) based on the prospect's identified problem severity, budget, and authority.

### Delivering a Great Demo
*   [ ] Cross-reference the proposed demo script with the prospect's pain points from the qualification call; flag any features included that do not address those specific pain points.
*   [ ] Review the plan for personalizing the demo environment (logos, data) and suggest enhancements.
*   [ ] Review the planned "magic moment" to ensure it will be surprising and delightful for the user's specific context.

### Pricing
*   [ ] Calculate a suggested price range based on the prospect's quantified cost of the problem.
*   [ ] Review the founder's proposed price point and challenge them if it seems too low.
*   [ ] Edit the 1-page PDF pricing/ROI overview to ensure it is persuasive for non-technical stakeholders (e.g., CFOs).
*   [ ] Roleplay or provide negotiation scripts based on the prospect's expected reaction to high pricing.

### Closing the Deal
*   [ ] Review the mapped procurement process and highlight which steps can be initiated immediately in parallel.
*   [ ] Review the communication strategy with the internal champion and suggest specific asks to help unblock legal/security.
*   [ ] Review the contract/order form drafts to confirm that timelines and scope of work are kept out of the main legal agreement.

### Managing the Implementation
*   [ ] Review the draft implementation roadmap to ensure every task has a clear owner (startup vs. customer).
*   [ ] Verify that the implementation plan is fully built *before* advising the founder to sign the contract.
*   [ ] Review the defined "habitual usage" metric to ensure it is a valid indicator of long-term success.
*   [ ] Advise on remediation steps if an implementation check-in reveals the customer is falling behind on their tasks.
