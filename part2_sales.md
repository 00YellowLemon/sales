# B2B Sales for Startups: Strategies, Tactics & Tradecraft (Session 2)

Notes extracted from Kent Summers' presentation on B2B sales.

## 1. Forecasting & Pipeline Management

* **The Challenge of Forecasting:** Forecasting is difficult for early-stage companies, but essential for answering investor questions about pipeline value and expected closings.
* **The "Weighted Pipeline" Method:** A simple, reliable method to measure the relative value (vs. absolute value) of a pipeline.
* **How to Calculate:** Multiply the potential value of a deal by a weighting percentage based on its stage in the sales process.
    * *Example:* 25% likelihood at "Buyer Interest" stage vs. 40% when "Timing is Known" and a business case is built.
* **Where Weightings Come From:** These percentages are the conversion rates between different stages of your sales funnel.
* **Start Tracking Now:** Conversion metrics take time (a year or more) to become reliable. Start capturing this data immediately to debug your process and inform future forecasts.
* **Variable Pricing:** For variable-priced offerings, factor in the potential deal value, stage, and timing (if known). If timing is unknown, do not put it in your forecast.
* **Quarterly vs. Monthly:** For long B2B sales cycles (8-10 months), forecast quarterly rather than monthly to avoid constantly pushing deals back.
* **Simplicity:** Keep the forecasting model simple; complex models yield diminishing returns.

## 2. Sales Economics & CAC (Cost of Customer Acquisition)

* **Sales is Expensive:** Sales is your most expensive investment in real and opportunity costs. You can't manage what you don't measure.
* **COCA (Cost of Customer Acquisition):** Subtract COCA from bookings to determine go-to-market margins.
* **Cost Increases with Depth:** The deeper a prospect gets into your sales process, the more expensive they become.
* **Track Costs per Stage:** Know your COCA at each stage to identify areas for improvement and scaling.
* **Pre-Sales Investment:** Measure the investment required *before* closing a sale. If an opportunity dies late in the process, understand the financial consequence.
* **Beware the "Free" Pilot:** Pilots are never free; they require significant time and resources. Ensure a pilot has clear entrance criteria, measurable success metrics, and a defined exit criteria (e.g., a commercial agreement).

## 3. Sales Roles, Attitude & Behavior

* **The Sales Facilitator:** The most mature sales skill level. The goal is to help prospects opt-in or opt-out of the sales process.
* **Empathy First:** Start with empathy. Understand the buyer's unique needs and pain points before offering solutions.
* **Build Trust:** Prove you have their best interests in mind, even if it means directing them elsewhere.
* **Establish Value:** Express the value of your offering in *their* terms and based on *their* outcome measures.
* **Competence Last:** Only after establishing empathy, trust, and value do you earn the right to discuss your competence, features, and capabilities.
* **Don't Educate for Free:** If a prospect needs basic education about what you do, they are not qualified.

## 4. Opting In/Out & Sales Tactics

* **The "Off-Ramp" Concept:** Provide prospects with easy ways to exit the sales process if they aren't a good fit. This saves time and builds trust.
* **Triage:** Disqualify people who have the appearance of interest but aren't truly qualified.
* **Testing Commitment:**
    * Ask about budget constraints.
    * Ask who else needs to be involved in the decision (to map the decision-making unit).
    * Ask what alternative solutions they are considering. If they say "none," they might not be serious.
* **Understanding ROI/Impact:** Ask about the financial impact of the problem. If they haven't measured it, they might just be stalling.
* **The "Brutal Call" Recovery:** Use a champion within the organization to understand objections and find ways to overcome them.
* **Dealing with Objections:** Sales is "debugging people's objections to spending money." When price is the *only* remaining objection, you are close to a deal.

## 5. Cold Outreach & Q&A Insights

* **Cold Outreach (Email/LinkedIn):** Use Occam's Razor: be brief. Anything beyond two sentences often won't be read. Get their attention quickly and use a clear, informal call to action (e.g., "let me know if you want to connect").
* **Storytelling in Sales:**
    * People buy based on emotion and instinct, then justify with logic.
    * Don't start with ROI; start with stories that appeal to emotion.
    * The core question every buyer has is: "Have you solved a similar problem for someone like me?"
    * Use anecdotes and case studies to answer this.
    * Recommended reading: *Mindset* by Carol Dweck (for the value of storytelling).
