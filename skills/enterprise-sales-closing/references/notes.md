# Enterprise Sales Closing Notes

*Based on Y Combinator's Startup School talk by Pete Koomen*

## Step 5: Pricing
- **No Simple Formula:** There isn't a formula. Pricing involves a lot of guessing in the beginning.
- **Leverage Qualification Questions:** Use the answers from the first call to guide pricing.
- **When to Wait:** If your product requires lots of work or customization to implement, do *not* quote a price until you fully understand exactly what the customer needs.
- **Pricing as an Experiment:** Treat each pricing conversation as an opportunity to test a price point and learn from the prospect's reaction.
  - *Tactic (Optimizely):* Left the "Enterprise plan" pricing unpublished to have flexibility to try a different price each time.
- **The Biggest Pricing Mistake:** Charging too little, or making it free in exchange for product feedback, out of fear of scaring customers away.
- **The Reality of High Prices:**
  - Higher prices help figure out if customers actually need your product. High prices make customers more serious.
  - When a customer *really* wants your product, it's hard to scare them away by quoting too high.
- **Enable Your Champion for Internal Conversations:**
  - The most important pricing conversations happen *without you in the room*.
  - Make their job easier: Give them slides or a PDF one-pager that explains how pricing works, includes an overview of the product, and details the benefits.
- **Actionable Advice:** Don't overthink it. Pick a number—ideally one that makes you a little uncomfortable—and pay attention to how the prospect reacts.

## Step 6: Closing the Deal
- **Definition:** Closing is a bunch of things that need to happen from the moment the customer decides they want the product to the moment they actually buy it.
- **The Procurement Process:**
  - **Big companies:** Formal processes including security, privacy, legal reviews, and sign-off from compliance.
  - **Smaller companies:** Less formal, but expect a redlining process with legal.
- **The Biggest Mistake:** Getting surprised. Thinking a deal is "done" when it is not, and then discovering it will take weeks/months.
- **How to Avoid Surprises:** Ask upfront: *How do you buy software? Who needs to sign off?*
- **Speeding up Procurement:**
  - Ask explicitly if there are steps (like a security questionnaire) you can start early and execute in parallel.
  - Keep legal documents simple (e.g., YC Common Paper).
  - Keep timelines and scope of work *out* of the legal contract (put in an order form).
- **Leverage Your Champion:**
  - Be in constant communication. Ask them for help when stuck.

## Step 7: Managing the Implementation
- **The Single Biggest Mistake:** Thinking that implementation is the customer's job.
- *The Real Cause:* The customer bought a *solution* to a problem. All the work required to get from product to solution is the founder's responsibility.
- **Best Practices for Implementation:**
  - Start asking marketing/engineering leaders about the work required to implement *very early* in the sales process.
  - Build detailed implementation plans well *before* a contract gets signed.
  - **Project manage it:** Treat the customer implementation the same way you would a high-priority project inside your own company. Put together a shared roadmap, assign owners to tasks, and set up regular check-in meetings.
- **The True End of the Funnel:** Your sales funnel only ends when your customer is using your product *habitually*.
