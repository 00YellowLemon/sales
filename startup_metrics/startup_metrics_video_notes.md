# AI Consulting in Practice: Enterprise AI Adoption and ROI

## Status of Enterprise AI Adoption
* Enterprises are increasingly adopting AI, with almost everyone using it at least a little bit.
* There has been a major inflection in AI adoption specifically in coding and software engineering.
* AI adoption is expanding beyond software engineering organizations, with other departments communicating and building with code.
* While mass automation across different functions hasn't occurred as some expected, there are significant shifts in the patterns of agent adoption.
* According to KPMG's quarterly pulse survey (companies over $1B revenue):
    * Full production agents in deployment jumped from 11% in Q1 to a higher percentage recently.
* Agent adoption has moved through the pilot/experimental phase faster than expected.
* Focus is shifting towards the human side of agents, specifically on upskilling, enablement work, and how humans interact with agents.
* Resistance to agents is decreasing as people dig in and interact with them in environments like sandboxes.

## Challenges in Scaling AI
* Many enterprises remain stuck in pilot and experimental phases.
* McKinsey's state of AI study shows:
    * Only 7% of organizations consider themselves fully at scale with AI and agents.
    * 62% are still in the experimenting or piloting phase.
* Contrary to expectations, larger organizations are scaling AI faster than smaller, more nimble companies.
* Adoption patterns are "jagged," with IT operations currently breaking out ahead of other functions.

## Leaders vs. Laggards in AI Adoption
* There is a significant bifurcation between leaders and laggards in AI adoption.
* Characteristics of leading companies:
    * Doing more with AI overall.
    * Thinking comprehensively and systematically about AI strategy as a whole.
    * Moving beyond spot experiments to pursue multiple initiatives simultaneously.
    * Focusing on advanced use cases like growing revenue, creating new capabilities, and developing new product lines, rather than just time savings and productivity.

## Expected AI Spend and ROI Outlook
* Spend on AI is expected to increase significantly:
    * KPMG survey: Expected AI spend in the next 12 months increased from $114M at the beginning of the year to $130M.
    * Deloitte study: Over 90% of organizations intend to increase AI spend in the next 12 months.
* Optimism around realizing ROI has increased substantially (KPMG CEO survey):
    * In 2024, the percentage of CEOs expecting ROI within 1-3 years increased to 67%, a significant pull-forward in expectations.
* Measuring ROI remains a challenge:
    * 78% of organizations believe ROI will become a bigger consideration in the coming year.
    * 78% state that traditional impact metrics and measures fail to accurately capture the impact of AI.

## The AI ROI Study Findings
* A study collecting self-reported ROI information on AI use cases generated over 3,500 use cases from 1,000+ organizations.
* **Impact Categories:** Time savings, increased output, improvement in quality, new capabilities, improved decision-making, cost savings, increased revenue, and risk reduction.
* **Current State of ROI:**
    * People are finding positive ROI right now.
    * A significant portion report high ROI (significant to transformational).
    * Only ~5% report negative ROI (which often means spend has exceeded gains so far, not necessarily that programs are failing).
* **Future Expectations:**
    * 67% expect high growth in ROI over the next year.
    * Even among teams with current negative ROI, 53% anticipate high growth.
* **Time Savings:**
    * Time savings is the dominant starting point, focusing on productivity, output, and quality.
    * Savings cluster between 1 to 10 hours per week (especially around 5 hours), equating to winning back 7-10 work weeks a year.
* **Organization Size Differences:**
    * Smaller organizations (1-50 people) are seeing more transformational benefits early compared to larger counterparts.
* **Role Variances:**
    * C-suite and leaders focus less on time savings and more on increased output and new capabilities.
    * Leaders are more optimistic, with 17% of leadership use cases reporting transformational impact already.
* **Industry Focus:**
    * Coding/software use cases show higher average ROI and lower negative ROI.
    * Risk reduction was the primary benefit for only 3.4% of use cases, but these were by far the most likely to achieve transformational ROI (often dealing with high volume/quantity tasks).
    * Healthcare and manufacturing sectors reported meaningfully higher impact on average compared to all organizations.
* **Automation and Agents:**
    * Use cases involving automation and agents wildly outperform others in terms of self-reported ROI, indicating the future trajectory of advanced use cases.
* **Systematic Approach Benefits:**
    * Organizations submitting more use cases generally reported better ROI, reinforcing the value of moving beyond single-spot experiments to a cross-organizational, systematic strategy.
