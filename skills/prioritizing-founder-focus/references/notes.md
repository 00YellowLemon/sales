# YC's Essential Startup Advice: Founder Focus

**Source:** [YC's essential startup advice](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice)

## Write Code, Talk to Users
- As companies grow, there are tons of potential distractions (conferences, dinners, meetings with VCs, corp dev, press coverage).
- **The Core Focus:** The most important tasks for an early-stage company are to write code and talk to users.
- **The Cycle:** Launch something $\rightarrow$ Talk to users to see if it serves their needs $\rightarrow$ Take feedback and iterate. These tasks should occupy almost all of a founder's time and focus. For great companies, this cycle never ends.

## Avoiding "Fake Work"
- Founders are often forced to choose between multiple directions.
- **Sam Altman's Rule:** It is nearly always better to take the more ambitious path.
- Founders frequently avoid tackling hard problems and focus on other things. Sam calls this "fake work" because it tends to be more fun than real work.

## Ignoring Competition
- It is difficult for founders not to obsess about actual and potential competition.
- **The Rule:** Spending *any* time worrying about competitors in the first year or two is nearly always a very bad idea.
- **Startup Death:** Startup companies always die of suicide, not murder.
- Competitive dynamics will eventually matter, but highly unlikely in the first year or two.

## Large Company Deals
- Founders are frequently tempted to chase big deals with large companies because it feels validating.
- Deals between large companies and tiny startups seldom end well: they take too long, cost too much, and often fail completely.
