# Enterprise Sales Prospecting Skill

## Overview
This skill provides instructions for identifying potential enterprise customers (prospecting) and formulating an effective outreach strategy (inbound and outbound), specifically for early-stage B2B/enterprise software startups.

## Prospecting Workflow
1. **Formulate the Sales Hypothesis**
   - Format: "Customer X has problem Y, and our product will help them solve it."
   - Action: Request the user to define their customer and the specific problem. Clarify who exactly the target user is.

2. **Identify Target Companies**
   - Use filtering criteria to qualify companies. (e.g., specific technologies used, industry sector, company size).
   - *Avoid*: Selling enterprise software to startups just because they are easier to talk to, if they don't actually need the product.
   - *Avoid*: Going bottom-up with a product that needs top-down adoption (e.g., products requiring coordination across many teams).

3. **Identify Target Humans**
   - Once companies are listed, find the correct individuals with decision-making authority or who directly experience the problem.

## Outreach Workflow
1. **Prioritize Inbound Demand**
   - Advise the user to generate inbound demand by:
     - Launching early and often.
     - Creating technical content or self-serve demos.
     - Engaging in online forums where target customers hang out.
     - Attending industry conferences and setting up meetings in advance.

2. **Conduct Outbound / Cold Outreach**
   - Start by looking for warm introductions (shared connections).
   - If cold emailing is necessary, follow these tactics:
     - Write each email by hand.
     - Make emails short and to the point.
     - Make the "ask" clear.
     - Explain *why* you are reaching out to this specific person.
   - *Golden Rule*: Only send emails that you yourself would be excited to read.

3. **Avoid the Major Anti-Pattern**
   - Do NOT talk to just anyone who will take the call.
   - This creates an illusion of progress. Target only those who have the problem, the budget, and the decision-making authority.
