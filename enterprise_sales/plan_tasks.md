# Enterprise Sales Planner Agent - Responsibilities and Tasks

Based on the Enterprise Sales playbook, this document outlines the high-level strategies and granular actions that an Enterprise Sales Planner Agent would be responsible for scheduling, managing, and guiding a founder through to close their first Enterprise customers.

## High-Level Phases

*   **Phase 1: Prospecting**
    *   Formulate a specific sales hypothesis identifying target customers and their problems.
    *   Identify target companies based on specific filters (e.g., technology stack, industry).
    *   Identify key individuals (humans) at those companies to reach out to.
*   **Phase 2: Outreach**
    *   Generate inbound demand through content, demos, and forum participation.
    *   Execute targeted outbound outreach, prioritizing warm introductions over cold emails.
    *   Ensure cold emails are personalized, short, and directed to the right decision-makers (avoiding "talking to anyone who will take your call").
*   **Phase 3: Qualification (The First Call)**
    *   Focus on listening and understanding the customer's problem, not pitching the product.
    *   Qualify prospects based on problem existence, budget, and decision-making authority.
    *   Schedule a follow-up demo only if the prospect is fully qualified.
*   **Phase 4: Delivering a Great Demo**
    *   Structure the demo as a story tailored to the specific prospect's use case and company.
    *   Focus on how the product solves the customer's previously identified problems rather than giving a feature tour.
    *   Create "magic moments" through extreme personalization.
*   **Phase 5: Pricing**
    *   Determine pricing iteratively based on qualification answers (cost of problem, budget).
    *   Avoid charging too little; use higher pricing to gauge serious intent.
    *   Provide materials to equip the internal champion for pricing conversations.
*   **Phase 6: Closing the Deal**
    *   Understand and map out the entire procurement process (legal, security, compliance) upfront to avoid surprises.
    *   Leverage the internal champion to unblock procurement hurdles.
    *   Keep contracts simple and separate from implementation timelines.
*   **Phase 7: Managing the Implementation**
    *   Take ownership of the implementation process from start to habitual product usage.
    *   Build detailed, shared implementation plans with tasks and owners before signing the contract.
    *   Project manage the deployment with regular check-ins to ensure success.

## Granular Actions & Tasks

### Prospecting
*   [ ] Draft a sales hypothesis formatted as: "Customer X has problem Y, and our product will help them solve it."
*   [ ] Create a target list of 50 companies using tools like BuiltWith to filter by relevant criteria.
*   [ ] Identify 2-3 specific human targets at each company on the list using LinkedIn Sales Navigator or Apollo.

### Outreach
*   [ ] Identify potential warm introductions for the top 10 target companies via LinkedIn connections.
*   [ ] Draft personalized cold emails for remaining targets ensuring the "ask" is clear and the email is short.
*   [ ] Schedule time to participate in relevant online forums or prepare technical content to generate inbound demand.
*   [ ] Pull the attendee list for an upcoming industry conference and request 5 pre-conference meetings.

### Qualification (The First Call)
*   [ ] Prepare a list of qualification questions to ask during the call (e.g., "How long have you had this problem?", "What is your budget?").
*   [ ] Set a reminder: Do NOT pitch the product on the first call; focus on listening.
*   [ ] Assess the prospect post-call: Do they have the problem, the budget, and the authority? (If no, disqualify).
*   [ ] Schedule the follow-up product demo call for qualified prospects.

### Delivering a Great Demo
*   [ ] Review notes from the qualification call to identify the prospect's main pain points.
*   [ ] Customize the demo environment with the prospect's logo, website, or specific data context.
*   [ ] Script the demo flow as a story, explicitly tying features back to the problems discussed on the first call.
*   [ ] Identify one "magic moment" feature to highlight during the demo.

### Pricing
*   [ ] Review qualification notes to estimate the financial cost of the prospect's problem.
*   [ ] Formulate a proposed price point (leaning higher to test willingness to pay).
*   [ ] Create a 1-page PDF pricing overview and ROI justification document to share with the internal champion.
*   [ ] Schedule a pricing review meeting with the prospect.

### Closing the Deal
*   [ ] Ask the prospect: "How does your organization buy software? Who needs to sign off?"
*   [ ] Send standard, simplified contract templates (e.g., Common Paper) to the prospect's legal team.
*   [ ] Send any required security/compliance questionnaires to get ahead of procurement blockers.
*   [ ] Schedule a weekly check-in with the internal champion specifically to track procurement progress.

### Managing the Implementation
*   [ ] Schedule an implementation planning meeting with the prospect's engineering/ops leaders prior to contract signature.
*   [ ] Draft a shared project tracking document (roadmap) with specific tasks, owners, and deadlines for implementation.
*   [ ] Schedule regular (e.g., weekly) check-in meetings for the duration of the implementation.
*   [ ] Define the specific metric or milestone that represents "habitual usage" to mark the end of the implementation phase.
