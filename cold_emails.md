# Detailed Notes: How To Convert Customers With Cold Emails | Startup School

**Speaker:** Aaron Epstein, Group Partner at YC
**Topic:** How to write cold emails that convert

These notes provide an exhaustive, chronological breakdown of the video content, specifically focused on capturing nuances to build sales agent skills in the future.

---

## 1. The Ultimate "Cold" Email Hack: Avoid It If Possible
- **The Core Strategy:** The absolute best way to succeed at "cold" outreach is to get a **warm intro**.
- **The "Why":** A warm intro yields a conversion rate **2x to 3x** higher than a regular cold email.
- **How to execute:**
    - Exhaust your network fully before giving up. Go through LinkedIn.
    - Check contacts: friends, friends of friends, current/former co-workers, school alumni networks, and past employer alumni networks.
    - **Sales Agent Skill Nuance:** The system should natively look for intermediate connection nodes before defaulting to a cold outreach. Turning over "every rock and stone" to find a connector is the prerequisite step to cold outreach.

## 2. Cold Outreach Baseline & The Funnel Approach
- **The Reality of Cold Emails:** Most cold emails are terrible, which means the bar is incredibly low. Merely being competent allows you to stand out.
- **Map Out Your Funnel:**
    - Start with your goal (e.g., getting 1 new customer) and **work backwards**.
    - **Example Funnel Breakdown:**
        - **Goal:** 1 Customer
        - Demo to Customer Conversion Rate (Assumed 10%): Need **10 Demos**
        - Response to Demo Conversion Rate (Assumed 25%): Need **40 Responses**
        - Open to Response Conversion Rate (Assumed 10%): Need **400 Opens**
        - Sent to Open Conversion Rate (Assumed 50%): Need to send **800 Emails**
- **The Volume Reality Check:**
    - You need to send a high volume of emails (e.g., 800) to get one customer, meaning you might need to send **dozens to 50+ per day**.
    - Sending "a handful" of emails periodically will not yield meaningful results.
- **Sales Agent Skill Nuance:**
    - **Goal setting:** The initial cold email's goal is *not* to make a sale. The goal is *only* to get them to the next step of the funnel (e.g., start a conversation, book a demo).
    - **Measurement:** Tracking step-by-step conversion is non-negotiable. Drop-offs at any point indicate where the copy/targeting needs intervention.
    - **Scaling Warning:** Conversion rates will *decrease* as you scale because you start exhausting your most ideal target persona. If manual rates are bad, scaling will only make them worse. Do it manually first to learn, then automate.

## 3. How to Increase Open Rates
- **The Secret to Open Rates:** The highest leverage activity to improve open rates is **Better Targeting**.
- **Quality over Quantity:** Sending 100 highly targeted emails will heavily outperform 1,000 untargeted emails. Untargeted emails are instantly deleted.
- **The Inbox View:** A recipient only sees the Sender Name, Subject Line, and a tiny preview of the first sentence.
    - **Sender Name:** Must be a *personal name* (e.g., "Aaron"), not a company name. It must feel like it came from a human.
    - **Subject Lines:** Keep them short, relevant, and interesting. They should feel like a friend wrote them. Do not sound "salesy".
        - *Good Examples:* "Quick question", "Can I get your advice", "Help a fellow founder", "Hello from [Company Name]"
- **Channel Nuances:**
    - Email is usually best.
    - LinkedIn is usually worse (often perceived as spammy depending on the industry).
    - Text messaging is highly personal and can feel invasive without explicit permission.
    - **Adaptability:** Different industries respond differently to channels; you must test to see if a channel feels "special" or "overwhelming" to your specific prospect type.
- **Finding Emails:** Use tools (Apollo.io, Hunter.io, Clearbit), guess standard formats (firstname.lastname@company.com), ask mutuals, or check their personal blogs/websites.

## 4. Brand & Credibility in Early Outreach
- **You Are The Brand:** When the company is small and unknown, the company brand will not open doors. *You* personally are the brand.
- **The Psychology of the Bet:** Recipients are not making a bet on your startup; they are taking a risky bet on *you personally*.
- **The "Over-the-Top" Care Tactic:** Give out your personal cell phone number in the email. Tell them to reach out "at any hour of the night." This proves you are going above and beyond to make them successful.

## 5. The 7 Principles of Effective Email Copy (How to Get Responses)

### Principle 1: Have a Focused, Specific Goal
- **The Rule:** Only have **one single outcome** in mind (e.g., respond to this email, click this link, make an intro).
- **The Danger of the Paradox of Choice:** Asking for multiple things causes cognitive overhead. If it feels hard or overwhelming, they will archive or delete it.
- **Ruthless Editing:** Every single word must drive towards that single goal. Delete anything that doesn't.

### Principle 2: Be Human
- **Emotional Language:** Humans talk about emotions. Use phrases like "I'd love to", "It would mean a lot", "I'd really appreciate", "You have no idea how much this helped." Bots and LLMs are bad at this.
- **Strategic Informality:** Avoid "Hello Mr. [Last Name]". Use "Hey [First Name]". No capitalization and minor typos can actually be *beneficial* because they signal a human wrote it, not an automated system.
- **Multimedia:** Use customized GIFs or videos. An animated GIF holding a whiteboard with the prospect's specific name on it proved highly effective because it showed real, personalized effort.
- **The Litmus Test:** Write how you talk to a friend. Read the email out loud to yourself or a co-founder. If it sounds awkward or unnatural to say out loud, rewrite it. Do not use generic templates with placeholders.

### Principle 3: Personalize deeply
- **Spam Radar:** People smell generic placeholders instantly. "Hi there" is an instant delete.
- **Specificity is Key:**
    - *Bad/Generic:* "Love what you're doing at [Company Variable]."
    - *Good/Specific:* "I'm a huge fan ever since you launched the photos category, it's my go-to resource." This proves actual research and product usage.
- **The "Uncommon Commonality":** This is the highest form of personalization. Find something you uniquely share with the prospect that is not broadly true of most people.
    - *Bad:* "We are both men, let's do business." (Too common, weird).
    - *Good:* "We went to the same college and had classes in the same specific building." (Builds instant connection and empathy).
    - *Effort Required:* Finding uncommon commonalities requires actual work (researching the company, the individual, and using their product).

### Principle 4: Keep It Short
- **The "Wall of Text" Rule:** An email that looks like a wall of text is an instant delete.
- **Mobile First:** Most emails are read on a phone. The goal is to make the email so brief and simple that the cognitive overhead is near zero, allowing them to reply in the moment. If it takes thought, it gets archived and forgotten.

### Principle 5: Establish Credibility
- **Name Dropping:** Mention YC, impressive former employers (e.g., Google), or top-tier universities.
- **Social Proof:** List impressive, recognizable current customers (e.g., Uber, Fitbit, GoPro) or direct competitors.
- **Value Add:** Provide interesting, proprietary industry data/trends to position yourself as an expert.
- **Shared Connections:** Mentioning mutual contacts creates a baseline of trust.

### Principle 6: Make It All About the Reader (Not You)
- **The "I/We" vs "You" Ratio:** Reframe all sentences starting with "I" or "We" to focus on "You" (the recipient).
- **The "Hero's Quest" Narrative:** Frame your startup's story as a quest to solve the recipient's exact problem. This is incredibly potent if you personally experienced the pain point. Tell them you are dedicating the next 10 years to solving this specific headache you both share.
- **Use Their Language:** Do not use your own internal marketing jargon. Ask your current users how they describe your product, and use *that exact phrasing* in cold emails and on your website.
- **Zero Demands:** Never demand a response by a certain date. You are in *their* inbox on *their* time; they owe you nothing.

### Principle 7: Have a Clear Call To Action (CTA)
- **Concrete Next Step:** Make the CTA extremely obvious (e.g., "Reply to let me know," "Click here," "Can you intro me?").
- **Formatting Hack:** The CTA must be its own **standalone sentence and paragraph** right before the sign-off. Readers often skim to the very end to figure out what the "ask" is. Make it instantly identifiable.

## 6. Follow-up Strategy
- **Persistence:** One email is rarely enough. People are busy. Plan to follow up 2, 3, or 4 times manually.
- **Spacing:** Leave a few days between emails to avoid being annoying.
- **Creativity:** Escalate creatively if ignored. (Example given: Subject line "Free Donuts" followed by physically delivering donuts to their office).
- **Grace:** Do not get angry or frustrated. They don't owe you anything. Move on and check back in a few months.

## 7. Email Tear-Downs (Real Examples)

### The Bad Examples
- **Example 1: The Non-Targeted Service Seller**
    - *Content:* Selling e-commerce physical shipping services.
    - *Critique:* The recipient's company sold downloadable digital assets. Because targeting was entirely wrong, no amount of good copywriting could have saved it. Targeting > Copy.
- **Example 2: The Generic "Extra Bucks" Partner**
    - *Content:* "Hey there... audience looks interesting to us... collaborate somehow to make extra bucks... PS if you don't want to hear from me let me know."
    - *Critique:*
        - Missing first name personalization.
        - Totally vague ("looks interesting", "collaborate somehow").
        - Unclear ask.
        - Unsigned.
        - The "PS" acts as an admission of being a spam blast, placing the burden on the recipient to unsubscribe.
- **Example 3: The "I, I, I" Freelancer**
    - *Content:* "I saw your profile... educated guess... I'm a freelancer... I have been helping... I was able to... I can help... I hope to share..."
    - *Critique:* Unnecessary explanation of how they found the contact. Entirely focused on the sender. Offers zero specific value to the recipient's actual problems.

### The Good Examples
- **Example 1: The Fellow Alumnus (Uncommon Commonality)**
    - *Subject:* "Go Terps"
    - *Content:* Mentioned specific campus building (Van Munching Hall). Mentioned being Ex-Google building a specific tool. Asked for feedback on a YC app, noting it was already pre-reviewed by others. Clear CTA for best email.
    - *Why it worked:* Perfect "uncommon commonality" building instant rapport. Built credibility (Google). Showed respect for time by doing pre-work. Extremely clear, low-friction CTA.
- **Example 2: The Exclusive Invitation (Aaron's own email)**
    - *Content:* Recruiting initial sellers for Creative Market.
    - *Why it worked:* Heavy use of formatting tricks (smiley faces) to feel informal. Written exactly how people talk ("oh and please don't share..."). Leveraged psychological triggers of **exclusivity and specialness** ("special link," "handpicked only," "welcome to the club," "super early"). Highly templated but felt bespoke.
- **Example 3: The "Smoothie Party" (Before & After)**
    - *Before:* "Hustling to achieve our demo day goal... forward me to your office manager... exchange for feedback... we got Uber using us..."
        - *Critique:* Good social proof, but heavily focused on the sender's needs (Demo Day goals) and unclear value to the recipient.
    - *After:* *Subject:* Smoothie Party. "I'd love to throw a free smoothie party... it's like happy hour but smoothies... all we ask is feedback... is there an afternoon I can come by..."
        - *Critique:* Instant framing shift. Makes it a "no-brainer" value-add for the recipient (free team party). Lowered friction drastically.

## 8. Final Golden Rules for Founders
- **Do It Manually First:** If you start with automation, you are doing it wrong. You must learn the nuances manually before scaling.
- **Founder Led:** Founders/CEOs MUST be the ones sending these early on. Recipients take founders drastically more seriously than interns or automated tools.
- **Block the Time:** If cold email is a growth strategy, you must physically block off hours per day to write dozens of them to get it right.
- **Small is an Advantage:** Being small allows you to give the dedicated, personalized, manual attention that large automated companies cannot mimic.
