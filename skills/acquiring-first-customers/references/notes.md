# Acquiring First Customers - Reference Notes

These notes capture practical, implementation-level knowledge extracted from the "How to Get Your First 10 Customers" YC talk by Max. They detail specific tactics, playbooks, and tool recommendations for early-stage sales.

## Phase 1: Understanding the Customer's Environment

Before using any tools or picking an outreach channel, founders must identify where the target customer spends their time.

*   **Avoid the Cold Email/LinkedIn Default Trap**: It is easy to default to email/LinkedIn because it can be done from a laptop and feels like "work." This works for buyers who live on computers (e.g., sales leaders).
*   **The Non-Laptop Buyer**: Many buyers (school administrators, property managers, truck dispatchers, construction site managers) do not sit at a laptop all day. Their inbox is not their primary workspace.
*   **Actionable Questions to Answer**:
    *   What does their average day look like?
    *   How often do they check their email?
    *   Do they go to conferences? Which ones?
    *   Are they active on Reddit or LinkedIn?
    *   Do they pick up the phone?
    *   Do they ask friends for recommendations or read industry newsletters?
*   **The Heuristic**: If a founder cannot answer the above questions concretely, they haven't spent enough time with real customers and should not attempt outreach yet.

## Phase 2: Customers 1 to 3 (The Warm Network)

The first few customers almost exclusively come from the founder's existing connections. These early adopters buy because they trust the founder, not just the product.

*   **First-Degree Network**: Talk to former colleagues, classmates, and friends in the industry.
*   **Second-Degree Network**: Use LinkedIn connections to find warm intros. Be specific when asking for an intro: Who do you want to meet, why would they care, and provide a forwardable email to make it easy for the referrer.
*   **Network Search Tools**: Use AI-powered network search tools (e.g., Happenstance) to search extended networks in natural language (e.g., "Find people who work on notification systems at big companies").
*   **Red Flag**: Using complex prospecting tools before exhausting the first and second-degree network means skipping the lowest-hanging fruit.

## Phase 3: Customers 4 to 10 (Unscalable Tactics)

This messy middle phase works because it requires the founder to do tedious, manual things that established companies won't do. The founder's personal effort signals that they care about the problem.

### In-Person Tactics
*   **Get in the Room**: Do not settle for Zoom. Show up in person. It is slower, awkward, and invites real-time rejection, but it is highly effective.
*   **Fly Out**: Fly out to meet executive buyers. Persistence (showing up repeatedly even after rescheduling or getting kicked out) often turns into closed accounts.
*   **Uninvited Visits**: Showing up at customer offices uninvited is a viable tactic if done persistently.

### The Conference Playbook
*   Small, industry-specific conferences have a higher conversion rate than cold emails.
*   **The Playbook**:
    1.  Set up Calendly with back-to-back 15-minute slots for the entire conference duration.
    2.  Email the attendee list multiple times before the event to fill slots.
    3.  Email the attendee list *during* the event to catch late-openers.
    4.  Stack meetings the entire day.

### Micro Events
*   Run founder dinners or happy hours for the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP).
*   Target 6 to 10 people, spending $50 to $100 per head.
*   Conversion rates are exceptionally high after breaking bread; attendees are unlikely to ignore follow-up emails and often go out of their way to help.

### Digital Communities (Where Pain is Public)
*   For consumer or SMB products, find where customers complain about the problem.
*   **Reddit**: Post videos of the product. While commenters may be harsh, "lurkers" (who don't comment) will often sign up quietly. Search old threads for complaints and DM commenters individually. Reddit threads index on Google, paying dividends over time.
*   **Other Platforms**: Facebook groups, Discord, YouTube comments, or niche industry message boards. Respond to complaints publicly (can be a full-time job for months).

## Phase 4: Creative Outreach Framing

When reaching out to strangers, framing the interaction as something other than a direct sales pitch can be highly effective. (Note: Only do this if genuinely open to the interaction type).

*   **The Mentorship Ask**: Reach out to industry CEOs asking for mentorship. Keep messages short and authentic. Flattery often leads to accepted calls, some of which convert to customers.
*   **User Research on Steroids**: Interview hundreds of people before building the product. Test one specific hypothesis per week. Build a pipeline of people who defined exactly what they needed.
*   **Free Work / Audits**: Offer startups free whiteboarding sessions for architectures that happen to require your product. Give a prospect something before asking for time (e.g., a quick API vulnerability scan, a walk-through of their mobile app with suggestions, a short compliance audit note).
*   **Paid Feedback**: For high-contract-value markets, offer to pay prospects (e.g., lawyers) a portion of their hourly fee ($100-$200) in exchange for product feedback. High conversion rates make the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) reasonable.

## Phase 5: Customers 10 to 50 (Scaling with Tools)

Only after securing 10-20 customers, refining the pitch, building case studies, and understanding the value prop should a founder use scaling tools.

*   **Apollo**: A lead database with email finding and a basic sequencer. The free tier is sufficient for building the first list. This is the primary starting point.
*   **Clay**: Used for building research and enrichment workflows with AI. Best for qualifying leads based on specific criteria (e.g., software stack, recent hiring, recent LinkedIn posts).
*   **LinkedIn Premium**: The richest source of fresh professional data. Best approach: Send a connection request *without* a message, then send a short DM once accepted.

## Cold Email Copy Best Practices
*   **Length**: Keep it under 75 words. Long emails look AI-generated and get ignored.
*   **Call to Action (CTA)**: The most important line. Be crystal clear on the ask (e.g., reply, call, 15-minute demo). Vague CTAs make the prospect assume it will waste their time.
*   **The Human Sanity Check**: Read the email out loud to a friend. If it sounds unnatural to speak, rewrite it. This removes "AI-sounding lies."
*   **Follow-up**: Follow up 3-4 times over a couple of weeks.
